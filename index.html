Dr. Keyan Ghazi-Zahedi

AI Leadership and Applied Research

Artificial intelligence from research into production, and the organisational work that makes it stick

Applied AI

The gap most AI work falls into

Plenty of models work in a notebook. Far fewer survive contact with real data, real users and real compliance requirements. The work I find interesting sits on that boundary: taking methods that are genuinely research-grade and making them reliable enough to run a process someone depends on. In practice that spans prediction, simulation, extraction, anomaly detection and generation, over text, images, video, audio, time series and structured records, learned with supervision where labels exist, without it where they do not, and by reinforcement where the problem is a sequence of decisions. Language models are one corner of that space, not the whole of it. Then the unglamorous parts, MLOps, infrastructure and data governance, that decide whether any of it can be deployed at all.

From the data towards the method

If all you know is a hammer, everything looks like a nail. Much of applied AI runs in exactly that direction: from the method someone is already comfortable with towards the problem, rather than from the problem towards the method that actually fits it. I work the other way round, and that starts with looking hard at the data itself. How is it spread out. Does it fall into distinct groups. How tangled is its structure. Which cases sit far outside the rest, and why. And how does all of that look along each individual measurement, as well as across them together. That picture is what makes one method sensible and another hopeless, and it is the step most projects skip. Skipping it is how you end up with something carefully built that could never have fitted the data in the first place.

Building the function, not just the model

A capable model is not a capability. Organisations that get somewhere with AI build the function around it: the team, the standards, the governance, and the working relationships with the parts of the business that will actually use the thing. What interests me is what that takes when an organisation has no prior AI capability at all, and has to develop one that survives contact with procurement, regulation and its own institutional habits. Research credibility is what makes technical judgement trustworthy; delivery is what makes it useful.

Research Areas

Morphological Computation

Quantifying Morphological Intelligence—how the body contributes to intelligent behavior. Developing information-theoretic measures to understand brain-body-environment interactions and their role in cognition.

Disinformation Research

Applying machine learning and natural language processing to detect and counter hate speech and disinformation on social media platforms using large-scale classification methods.

Neural Networks

Developing novel neural network architectures including self-regulating neurons with adaptive synaptic plasticity, SO(2)-networks as neural oscillators, and recurrent neural networks for robot control.

Research Focus

Quantifying Morphological Intelligence

Can we measure how much of an agent's behaviour is controlled by the brain and how much results from exploiting the physical properties of its morphology? I develop information-theoretic methods to quantify these contributions, enabling a deeper understanding of embodied cognition and more efficient robot design.

Disinformation Detection

In an era where artificial intelligence can generate compelling text, realistic images, convincing audio, and even fabricated video, the detection of disinformation has become one of the most pressing challenges of our time. Malicious actors can now deploy sophisticated disinformation campaigns at unprecedented scale, threatening democratic processes, public health, and social cohesion. My research applies machine learning and natural language processing to detect hate speech and disinformation, while also studying the dynamics of how counter speech can effectively combat the spread of harmful content online.
