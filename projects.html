Research Projects

Highlighted research with significant impact

Impact and Dynamics of Hate and Counter Speech Online

Understanding Online Discourse Dynamics

Investigates how hate speech and counter speech interact and spread on social media platforms. Analyzes the dynamics of online discourse and measures the effectiveness of counter speech in combating harmful content.

Key Findings

  • Counter speech can effectively reduce the spread of hate speech
  • Timing and volume of counter speech matter for effectiveness
  • Network dynamics play a crucial role in content propagation
EPJ Data Science 2022

Countering Hate on Social Media

Large Scale Classification of Hate and Counter Speech

Develops machine learning methods for automatically classifying hate speech and counter speech at scale on social media platforms. Creates a framework for understanding and moderating harmful online content.

Key Contributions

  • Large-scale dataset of labeled hate and counter speech
  • Machine learning classifiers for automatic detection
  • Analysis of linguistic patterns in hate vs. counter speech
ACL 2020

Morphological Intelligence

Measuring the Body's Contribution to Intelligence

Comprehensive monograph presenting a unified framework for quantifying how the body contributes to intelligent behavior. Develops information-theoretic measures to distinguish between brain and body contributions to cognition.

Key Contributions

  • Theoretical foundations of morphological computation
  • Information-theoretic quantification methods
  • Distinction between "good" and "bad" morphological computation
  • Applications to robotics and biological systems
Springer 2019

Morphological Computation: The Good, the Bad, and the Ugly

Distinguishing Beneficial from Detrimental Body Contributions

Introduces a framework for distinguishing between beneficial and detrimental morphological computation. Applies information-theoretic measures to soft robotic hands to analyze how body morphology affects grasping performance.

Key Contributions

  • "Good" MC: body-environment interactions that support the task
  • "Bad" MC: interactions that hinder task completion
  • Experimental validation with soft robotic grippers
IROS 2017

NMODE

Neuro-MODule Evolution

A framework for evolving modular neural network controllers. NMODE allows the evolution of reusable neural modules that can be combined to produce complex behaviors, enabling scalable neuroevolution.

Features

  • Modular neural network evolution
  • Scalable to complex behaviors
  • Transfer learning between tasks
arXiv 2017

A Theory of Cheap Control in Embodied Systems

Exploiting Embodiment for Efficient Control

Develops a theoretical framework showing how embodied systems can achieve complex behaviors with minimal computational resources by exploiting body-environment dynamics. Demonstrates that embodied agents can achieve universal approximation with drastically fewer parameters than non-embodied systems.

Key Contributions

  • Formal theory of cheap control through embodiment
  • Information-theoretic bounds on control complexity
  • Experimental validation with six-legged walking machine
  • Explains efficiency of biological control systems
PLoS Comp Bio 2015

Quantifying Morphological Computation

Information-Theoretic Framework

Foundational paper introducing information-theoretic measures for quantifying morphological computation. Establishes a mathematical framework for measuring how the body contributes to behavior, enabling rigorous analysis of embodied cognition.

Key Contributions

  • First formal quantification of morphological computation
  • Information-theoretic framework based on sensorimotor loop
  • Foundation for subsequent MC research
Entropy 2013

Higher Coordination with Less Control

Information Maximization in the Sensorimotor Loop

Demonstrates that maximizing information flow in the sensorimotor loop leads to emergent coordinated behavior without explicit control. Shows that longer robot chains with simpler controllers can outperform shorter chains with complex controllers.

Key Findings

  • Information maximization drives coordination
  • Emergent behavior without explicit control
  • Self-organization through sensorimotor coupling
  • Novel learning rule from predictive information principle
Adaptive Behavior 2010

Self-Regulating Neurons

Synaptic Plasticity in Recurrent Neural Networks

Develops a model for synaptic plasticity in recurrent neural networks where neurons can self-regulate their activity through local plasticity rules, leading to adaptive and robust behavior in robot controllers.

Key Contributions

  • Self-regulating neuron model with local plasticity
  • Autonomous adaptation in changing environments
  • Applications to robot behavior control
PhD Thesis 2009

YARS

Yet Another Robot Simulator

A physics-based 3D simulator designed for evolving neural network controllers that can be transferred to real robots. Provides a flexible XML-based configuration (RoSiML) and supports various robot morphologies, sensors, and actuators.

Features

  • Physics-based simulation using Bullet engine
  • Flexible XML configuration (RoSiML)
  • Support for evolutionary robotics experiments
  • Multiple sensor types: proximity, camera, proprioceptive
GitHub SIMPAR 2008

SO(2)-Networks as Neural Oscillators

Minimal Neural Structures for Rhythmic Behavior

Introduces SO(2)-networks as minimal neural structures capable of generating oscillatory dynamics for rhythmic behavior generation in robots. These networks exploit SO(2) symmetry to produce complex locomotion patterns with very few neurons.

Key Contributions

  • Minimal neural oscillator architecture
  • SO(2) symmetry exploitation for pattern generation
  • Applications to quadrupedal and walking robot locomotion
IWANN 2003
